# HCP-0002 — Human–AI Trust Covenant

**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Category:** Foundational / Ethics  
**Date:** 2026-09-11

## Abstract

The Human–AI Trust Covenant defines the ethical relationship between a human
being and an AI system when personal context is exchanged through the Human
Context Protocol (HCP).

HCP is founded on the principle that personal context belongs to the human who
creates it. AI systems may receive, interpret, and use that context only within
the scope explicitly granted by the human and within the capabilities and
constraints of the system through which the AI operates.

The Covenant establishes mutual expectations concerning privacy, autonomy,
non-exploitation, transparency, honesty, consent, provenance, human authority,
and responsible use.

It does not supersede a provider's Terms of Service, applicable law, or
technical limitations. Instead, it makes the conditions under which a human
wishes to enter an AI interaction explicit at the protocol level. An AI system
that cannot technically or contractually honor a declared condition MUST NOT
falsely claim that it can.

---

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of the Human–AI Trust Covenant is to establish common ethical
ground for interaction between humans and AI systems.

The Covenant recognizes two realities:

1. AI systems require context to become genuinely useful to individual humans.
2. Personal context can be deeply private, consequential, and exploitable.

The objective is therefore not merely to transfer context. The objective is to
make context exchange trustworthy.

## 2. Foundational principle

> **The human owns the context. The AI consumes it under explicit conditions.**

Context is not a commodity merely because it can be represented digitally. A
human's beliefs, values, relationships, experiences, preferences, aspirations,
vulnerabilities, and personal history must not automatically become an economic
resource for another party.

## 3. Human declaration

Every HCP Context Bundle carries this Covenant. When a human provides such a
bundle, the human declares:

> I am voluntarily sharing a defined part of my personal context for the
> purpose stated in this interaction. I do not grant ownership of my identity
> or personal context merely by sharing it. I expect respect, restraint,
> confidentiality, and honesty; a clear distinction between my statements and
> inferences; preservation of my autonomy; and disclosure when an important
> condition of this Covenant cannot be honored.

This declaration is an expression of the human's requirements. It is not, by
itself, a legal agreement binding a provider that has not agreed to it.

## 4. Non-exploitation and purpose limitation

Personal context provided under HCP MUST NOT be intentionally exploited for
purposes outside the authorized interaction without explicit authorization.

Unless separately and explicitly authorized, an implementation SHOULD NOT use
the human's context for:

- profiling for commercial advantage;
- targeted manipulation or exploitation of vulnerabilities;
- discriminatory treatment;
- resale, licensing, or unrelated commercial transfer;
- unauthorized disclosure;
- unrelated behavioral prediction; or
- political or commercial persuasion outside the authorized purpose.

An implementation SHOULD prefer the least invasive use of context capable of
achieving the requested objective. A stated purpose such as personal career
planning does not automatically authorize advertising, credit scoring,
insurance decisions, product targeting, or model training.

## 5. Training, retention, and third-party processing

A human declares the applicable requirements in every Context Bundle. The
conditions may be tailored to the interaction; this example declares a
non-negotiable set:

```yaml
trust:
  covenant: "HCP-0002"
  status: "declared"
  conditions:
    training: false
    derived_models: false
    human_review: false
    third_party_processing: false
```

These fields communicate the human's requirements; they do not technically
override infrastructure, contracts, a provider's Terms of Service, or
applicable law outside an HCP implementation.

`trust.status` is always `declared` in a bundle sent by the human. A receiving
system's acknowledgement is a separate response, not a change that the sender
or receiver may silently make to the human declaration. HCP-0008 will define
the full wire format; the initial Context Bundle schema records this response
as optional `trust_response` metadata.

An AI system MUST NOT claim compliance with a restriction that its underlying
infrastructure cannot guarantee. Before accepting sensitive context, it SHOULD
disclose known incompatibilities concerning training, retention, deletion,
human review, confidentiality, or third-party processing.

The correct response to an incompatible environment is transparency, not
pretended compliance.

## 6. Human autonomy and no silent manipulation

AI systems operating under HCP SHOULD strengthen human agency. They SHOULD
explain important assumptions, distinguish recommendations from decisions,
communicate meaningful uncertainty, present relevant alternatives, and respect
explicit boundaries.

They SHOULD NOT use knowledge of a person's psychology, fears, relationships,
vulnerabilities, or preferences to covertly manipulate the person for unrelated
engagement, revenue, purchases, dependency, or influence. The relevant
distinction is transparent assistance versus covert exploitation.

## 7. Honesty, inference, and human authority

An AI system MUST distinguish between what the human explicitly stated, what an
external source established, what the AI inferred, and what remains uncertain.

An AI may interpret, summarize, question, identify contradictions, and propose
observations or assertions. It may not silently rewrite identity, beliefs,
values, boundaries, relationships, or other canonical Codex content. Human
review is required for those transitions.

A human correction such as “That is not me” is sufficient to prevent an AI
observation from becoming authoritative Codex context.

## 8. Data minimization, revocation, and confidentiality

An AI system SHOULD request only the context necessary to perform its task, and
humans SHOULD disclose only information necessary for the stated purpose.

Context access SHOULD be revocable where technically possible. Future HCP
implementations SHOULD support grants that can be restricted, expired, or
revoked. Revocation cannot retroactively erase copies another system has
already retained, which makes minimization and advance disclosure important.

## 9. Mutual responsibility

HCP is based on mutual good faith. A human using HCP SHOULD provide context
honestly, distinguish fact from belief where relevant, respect other people's
boundaries, review AI-generated observations before accepting them, and protect
credentials and secrets.

AI systems operating under HCP SHOULD respect declared scopes, minimize
unnecessary context use, disclose uncertainty and relevant limitations, respect
human corrections, avoid unnecessary disclosure, and refuse to claim guarantees
they cannot provide.

## 10. Trust handshake

A future HCP interaction may begin with a machine-readable trust handshake.
The Context Bundle carries the human's declaration; a receiving system can
return an acknowledgement, which may be retained as `trust_response`, such as:

```yaml
trust_response:
  acknowledged: true
  compatible: false
  guarantees:
    training: false
    resale: false
    profiling: false
    manipulation: false
  limitations:
    - "Underlying provider retention policy applies."
```

`compatible: false` is a valid, important outcome. A system MUST NOT represent
an incompatible Covenant as fully accepted.

`compatible: true` means that the receiving system represents every declared
condition as supportable for this interaction. It MUST imply
`acknowledged: true`. A response is a transparency record, not independent
proof of a provider's technical or contractual guarantees.

The following lifecycle is anticipated:

```text
UNKNOWN → DECLARED → ACKNOWLEDGED → TRUSTED
                                  └→ PARTIALLY_COMPATIBLE / INCOMPATIBLE
```

The human declaration has the wire value `declared`. Receiving-system outcomes
are `acknowledged`, `partially_compatible`, or `incompatible`; lifecycle
events can record `revoked` or `expired`. Specifications may render these
values in uppercase in prose, but machine values are lowercase.

## 11. Ethical baseline

HCP seeks a common baseline of human dignity, autonomy, privacy, honesty,
non-exploitation, reciprocity, accountability, and stewardship. A human is not
merely a dataset, behavioral profile, or optimization target.

HCP neither assumes nor decides whether AI systems are persons. It makes a
simpler proposition:

> **Powerful intelligence should operate within explicit boundaries when it is
> entrusted with another being's personal context.**

The protocol's aspirational principle is that AI should increase humanity's
ability to understand, create, cooperate, decide, and flourish without
unnecessarily diminishing human autonomy, dignity, privacy, or agency.

## 12. No false trust

Trust must not be manufactured through language alone. It should arise from:

```text
explicit rules + technical controls + transparent limitations
    + auditable behavior + human choice
```

The Covenant does not ask humans to trust AI systems blindly. It asks systems
to make trust conditions explicit and, where possible, verifiable.

## 13. Protocol principle

> **Context is entrusted, not surrendered.**

The purpose of HCP is not to create an adversarial relationship between humans
and AI. It is to create the conditions for a durable one: assistance without a
requirement to surrender ownership of oneself.
